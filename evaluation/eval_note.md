## What each metric is actually measuring here

- **Groundedness** (`evaluator/groundedness.py`): fraction of risk-item text
  that is a verbatim substring of the source chunk it's cited from. In mock
  mode, risk items are produced by `RiskExtractor`, which extracts whole
  sentences directly out of the Risk Factors chunk — so groundedness is
  trivially 1.0 by construction. This isn't a meaningful test of the metric
  itself; it will become a real test once `MOCK_LLM=false` runs against the
  live Claude API, where risk text is model-paraphrased rather than
  extracted verbatim.
- **Sentiment agreement** (`evaluator/agreement.py`): predicted tone
  (`scoring/lexicon_scorer.py`, word-count based) vs. `data/gold_labels.json`
  (hand-labeled from reading each filing as an analyst would). All three
  agree here, but see the FILING_001 note below — the agreement is closer
  than it looks.
- **Coherence** (`evaluator/coherence.py`): bullet-count/length sanity
  checks (2 highlights, 2-3 risks, ≥15 chars each). This is the one metric
  that actually failed on the current corpus.

## Error case: FILING_003 coherence failure

`FILING_003`'s Risk Factors section is a single sentence ("Continued softness
in consumer spending could further reduce store traffic."). `RiskExtractor`
splits a chunk into sentences and ranks them by uncertainty-word density —
with only one sentence available, it can only return one risk item, so
`coherence_score` correctly flags "Expected 2-3 risks, got 1" (score 0.75/1.0
instead of 1.0).

This is a genuine limitation, not a bug: a sentence-count-based extractor is
fundamentally unable to produce 2-3 distinct risk bullets when the source
section itself only contains one distinct risk statement. Two ways to
address it if this recurs on real filings: (a) fall back to pulling
uncertainty-scored sentences from adjacent sections (e.g. Outlook) when Risk
Factors is too short, or (b) treat sub-1.0 coherence on thin sections as
expected/acceptable rather than a defect, and only flag it when it happens on
filings with substantive Risk Factors text. We left this as an observed,
logged limitation rather than "fixing" it by padding FILING_003's Risk
Factors artificially, since that would hide a real edge case the extractor
needs to handle before Phase 5's real-PDF swap (real filings will have
plenty of terse or single-paragraph Risk Factors sections).

## Borderline case: FILING_001 tone

FILING_001 has real positive signals (revenue +17%, margin +, "record order
backlog") alongside a disclosed patent litigation and an explicitly "volatile"
outlook. The lexicon scorer counted 5 positive words, 2 negative words, and
6 uncertainty words (`could`, `if`, `may`, `uncertain`, `could`, `volatile`)
across the full filing text — uncertainty count exceeds both positive and
negative counts, so it correctly falls into "cautious" per
`LexiconScorer.score_tone`'s priority rule (uncertainty dominance checked
before positive/negative comparison). The gold label was set to "cautious"
for the same reason a human analyst would: qualitative forward-looking risk
language dominates over a single quarter's growth numbers. This agreement
held, but it's a close call the lexicon approach could easily get wrong on a
filing with slightly fewer uncertainty words — worth watching as the corpus
grows.

## What's not yet evaluated

- **Real Claude API path** (`MOCK_LLM=false`) is implemented
  (`summarizer/claude_client.py::_call_api`) but has not been run for this
  submission — no `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` was available in this environment. The
  mock path exercises the full pipeline and all proxy metrics end-to-end, but
  groundedness in particular will only be a meaningful signal once real
  (paraphrased, not extracted) LLM output is scored against it.
- **Corpus size**: 3 synthetic filings. Enough to exercise all three tone
  labels and produce one real coherence failure, but too small to draw
  statistical conclusions about metric reliability — treat the 100% agreement
  figure as "no disagreements observed yet," not "the scorer is accurate."
